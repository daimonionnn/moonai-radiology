# From click to measurement: strengthening oncology in MOONAI

Yes, better and more specialized models exist. For oncology, the biggest gap in MOONAI is **universal one-click lesion segmentation combined with lesion tracking over time**. Today that gap is filled by freely available models with downloadable weights: **ULS+** (Radboud, 2026, Dice 0.78 on the ULS23 test set), **LesionLocator** (DKFZ, lesion segmentation plus longitudinal tracking) and **nnInteractive** (winner of the CVPR 2025 interactive 3D challenge, with clients for OHIF and 3D Slicer). The second layer consists of validated models aimed at a specific organ or modality: **LesionTracer** for whole-body PET/CT (CC BY 4.0), **PI-CAI** for prostate bpMRI, where AI reached AUROC 0.91 versus 0.86 for 62 radiologists, **PANORAMA/PanDx** for pancreatic cancer, the **BrainLesion `brats`** package for gliomas, meningiomas and brain metastases, **MAMA-MIA** for breast tumors on DCE-MRI and **Sybil** for lung cancer risk from LDCT. In anatomical segmentation, the cheapest and safest step is to deploy **TotalSegmentator v2** as the main automatic segmenter, not just as a reference. That retires the outdated wholeBody_ct_segmentation and swin_unetr_btcv and adds MR anatomy. There are three main weaknesses. No current open model can automatically *find* all lesions across the whole body. Open weights are missing for bone metastases, lymph nodes and head and neck. Weight licences range from CC BY 4.0 to CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and custom "research-only" terms. For non-commercial research in a Slovak hospital practically all of these models are usable, **but no open model carries a CE mark under the MDR**. Their outputs must therefore stay labeled as research and outside clinical decision-making.

## Gap number one: click-to-segment lesions and RECIST measurement

The current MONAI Zoo set covers oncology only narrowly: lung nodules (lung_nodule_ct_detection), old BraTS gliomas, pancreas and pathology. VISTA3D does contain 132 CT classes including 7 tumor types and an interactive point mode ([Project-MONAI/VISTA](https://github.com/Project-MONAI/VISTA/tree/main/vista3d)), but it is not a model built for general lesions. For an oncology institute the most valuable tool is one that, when you click any lesion in the lung, liver, a lymph node, bone or kidney, returns a 3D mask together with the long and short axis. That is exactly what the ULS23 challenge family of models is for.

The original **ULS23 baseline** is a semi-supervised 3D nnU-Net. On the test set it reached a mean Dice of **0.703 ± 0.240** and a long-axis SMAPE of 11.2 % ([ULS23, MedIA 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841525000738)). Performance varies considerably by lesion type: lung 0.754, kidney 0.724, lymph nodes 0.698, liver 0.650, bone 0.648, pancreas 0.616 and colon only 0.518 ([Grand Challenge ULS23](https://grand-challenge.org/algorithms/universal-lesion-segmentation-uls23-baseline/)). The weights (2.5 GB) are on Zenodo under **CC BY 4.0** ([Zenodo 10777532](https://zenodo.org/records/10777532)). Its successor **ULS+** (Radboud, January 2026) added more training data, click-position augmentation and TTA. On the ULS23 test set it raised Dice from 0.74 to **0.78**, robustness from 0.81 to 0.86, and on the longitudinal CT subset from 0.68 to **0.79**. It ranks first on the ULS23 leaderboard ([arXiv 2601.02988](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02988v1)). The code is Apache-2.0, built on nnU-Net v2 ResEnc-L, and the weights are on Hugging Face `nielsRocholl/ULS_plus` and Zenodo 17937197 ([GitHub DIAGNijmegen/oncology-uls-plus](https://github.com/DIAGNijmegen/oncology-uls-plus)). **The ULS+ weight licence has not been verified yet.** It must be checked directly on Zenodo before the model is deployed. Both ULS versions are prompt-based models, not detectors. They segment only the lesion that is clicked and cannot handle a lesion larger than their field of view.

**LesionLocator** (DKFZ, CVPR 2025) performs zero-shot lesion segmentation from a point or a 3D box. On top of that it adds a `LesionLocator_track` mode that propagates the mask from the baseline study to follow-up studies. According to the authors it won the longitudinal lesion tracking task of autoPET IV ([GitHub MIC-DKFZ/LesionLocator](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/LesionLocator)). The code is GPL-3.0. The checkpoint is 14.1 GB and licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** ([Zenodo 15174217](https://zenodo.org/records/15174217)). The repository warns about high RAM usage when resampling to the fine 1.0 × 0.8 × 0.8 mm grid.

**nnInteractive** (DKFZ) is a general prompt-based 3D segmenter for CT, MR and PET. It accepts points, scribbles, boxes and lasso. About **10 GB VRAM** is recommended; the weights are roughly 400 MB and licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. Its main practical advantage is ready integration: official napari and MITK, community 3D Slicer, ITK-SNAP and **OHIF (OHIF-AI from CCI Bonn)**, plus a client/server mode with no local GPU needed on the client side ([GitHub MIC-DKFZ/nnInteractive](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnInteractive)). For MOONAI this means nnInteractive can run as a separate service next to MONAI Label without writing a custom wrapper. The third tool in this category is **MedSAM2** (bowang-lab). It has dedicated CTLesion and MRI_LiverLesion checkpoints and propagates a mask through the volume from a box on the middle slice — in practice from a RECIST diameter. Its weights, however, are explicitly "for research and education" only ([HF wanglab/MedSAM2](https://huggingface.co/wanglab/MedSAM2)).

An important caveat for follow-up: the Radboud team showed that ULS-type prompt models produce **unstable segmentations when the prompt or crop shifts**, and explicitly calls this a problem for longitudinal analysis ([arXiv 2507.19230](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19230v1)). A volume change between two studies may therefore partly be an artifact of where the radiologist clicked. This argues for LesionLocator_track, which propagates the mask instead of starting from a new click, and for local verification of measurement reproducibility.

For completeness: **no open automatic detector of all lesions across the whole body from 2025–2026 was found**. DeepLesion-era detectors (ULDor, DKMA-ULD) have no published weights ([arXiv 2203.06886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.06886)), and nnDetection's pretrained models are still "Coming Soon" ([GitHub MIC-DKFZ/nnDetection](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnDetection)). Automatic "find everything" can today only be assembled in MOONAI from per-organ and per-modality models.

## Organ-specific models: where validated weights exist and where they are missing

The second layer of oncology models is automatic and tied to a specific indication. Only three have strong multi-center evidence of radiologist-level performance: PI-CAI, PANORAMA and Sybil. The rest are challenge results without second-reader studies. Overview of the main candidates:

| Indication | Model | Performance (per source) | Weight licence | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-body PET/CT (FDG, PSMA) | LesionTracer (autoPET III) | CV Dice 68.40 vs 57.61 default nnU-Net | **CC BY 4.0** | Best-validated open set for PET/CT |
| Prostate bpMRI | PI-CAI nnU-Net (semi-supervised) | AUROC 0.91 vs 0.86 radiologists | Code Apache-2.0, weights unstated | Siemens/Philips only, surface coils |
| Pancreas CECT (PDAC) | PanDx (PANORAMA 1st place) | AUROC 0.9263, AP 0.7243 | Unverified | Code public |
| Brain (gliomas, meningiomas, metastases) | BrainLesion `brats` | BraTS winning algorithms | Code Apache-2.0, containers unstated | Docker, 4 sequences |
| Breast DCE-MRI | MAMA-MIA nnU-Net baseline | Dice 0.762 | **CC BY-NC 4.0** | Trained on US data |
| Lung LDCT (risk) | Sybil | AUC (1 year) 0.86–0.94 | **MIT** | Does not segment |
| Liver CT/MR | TotalSegmentator `liver_lesions(_mr)` | Not stated | **Disputed, see below** | Smaller training set |
| Lung nodules CT | TotalSegmentator `lung_nodules` | Not stated | Apache-2.0 | Complements the MONAI detector |

**Whole-body PET/CT.** LesionTracer (DKFZ, nnU-Net ResEncL with organ supervision) won the model-centric category of autoPET III. In cross-validation it reached Dice 68.40 versus 57.61 for a standard nnU-Net ([arXiv 2409.09478](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09478v2)). The final checkpoint (3.8 GB) is licensed **CC BY 4.0** and the code Apache-2.0 ([Zenodo 14007247](https://zenodo.org/records/14007247)). Beware of tracer dependence: another autoPET III team reached Dice 76.90 % for FDG but only 61.33 % for PSMA ([arXiv 2409.12155](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12155v1)). The final autoPET IV winners could not be found.

**Prostate.** The PI-CAI study (Lancet Oncology 2024) trained AI on 9,207 exams and tested it on 1,000 exams from 4 centers. The AI reached **AUROC 0.91 versus 0.86** for 62 radiologists and, at the same specificity, produced 50.4 % fewer false positives ([PubMed 38876123](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38876123/)). The algorithm's README, however, explicitly states that the model was trained only on **Siemens and Philips scanners with surface coils**, is not compatible with endorectal coils or other vendors, and should not be used in an external setting without further evaluation ([GitHub picai_nnunet_semi_supervised_gc_algorithm](https://github.com/DIAGNijmegen/picai_nnunet_semi_supervised_gc_algorithm)). The weights have no direct download link. The realistic route is exporting the container from Grand Challenge or retraining from `picai_baseline` on public data.

**Pancreas.** The PANORAMA challenge winner (about 3,000 portal-venous CECT scans), PanDx, reached a test AUROC of 0.9263, and its code is public ([arXiv 2503.10068](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10068v2)). In the PANORAMA reader study the AI reached AUROC 0.92 versus 0.88 for radiologists ([JMAI](https://jmai.amegroups.org/article/view/11400/html)). Functionally, PanDx would replace or complement pancreas_ct_dints_segmentation, which is focused on segmentation.

**Brain.** The BrainLesion `brats` package bundles the best algorithms from the BraTS challenges as Docker containers. It covers adult gliomas (pre- and post-treatment), meningiomas, metastases and other tasks. It requires all 4 sequences t1c/t1n/t2f/t2w and the NVIDIA Container Toolkit ([GitHub BrainLesion/BraTS](https://github.com/BrainLesion/BraTS)). The container licence is not stated and probably follows the individual teams' terms. Functionally it extends the old brats_mri_segmentation, which covers only gliomas, with metastases and meningiomas. That matters for an oncology institute.

**Breast (MRI).** MAMA-MIA provides a downloadable nnU-Net for primary tumor segmentation on DCE-MRI with Dice **0.762** under **CC BY-NC 4.0** ([GitHub LidiaGarrucho/MAMA-MIA](https://github.com/LidiaGarrucho/MAMA-MIA)). The MAMA-MIA challenge (MICCAI 2025) tested on more than 570 cases from 3 European centers ([MAMA-MIA](https://www.ub.edu/mama-mia/)), but the baseline was trained only on US data. Expect domain shift.

**Lung.** Sybil predicts lung cancer risk at 1 to 6 years from a single LDCT. The one-year AUC is 0.92 (NLST), 0.86 (MGH) and 0.94 (Chang Gung) ([PMC10419602](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10419602)). It is MIT-licensed and installs via pip ([GitHub Sybil](https://github.com/reginabarzilaygroup/Sybil)). Much of the short-term signal, however, comes from visible nodules. Excluding them, the six-year AUC drops to **0.69** ([PMC10240246](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10240246)). Sybil also expects axial LDCT from the clavicles to the abdomen, so it is not validated on diagnostic contrast CT. LUNA25 showed an AUC of 0.78 versus 0.70 for 65 radiologists for nodule malignancy on European screening data ([RSNA ryai.260179](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/ryai.260179)). Official weights were not confirmed.

**Liver and the licence conflict.** No dedicated open model for colorectal liver metastases exists. The best Dice in the 2026 CRLM-CT-Seg benchmark was only 0.620, and weight release is not confirmed ([arXiv 2604.07999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07999)). The open option is the TotalSegmentator tasks `liver_lesions` and `liver_lesions_mr`. **The notes disagree on their licence, however.** The first oncology note says `liver_lesions` is among the restricted tasks that require a licence (free for non-commercial use). The later and more direct README check in the oncology-gaps note, and independently the CT/MR segmentation note, list it among the **open Apache-2.0 tasks**. According to them the restricted tasks are heartchambers_highres, appendicular_bones, tissue_types, brain_structures, face, thigh_shoulder_muscles, coronary_arteries, aortic_sinuses and brain_aneurysm ([GitHub wasserth/TotalSegmentator](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator)). The later check carries more weight, but the README has changed between versions. **The `liver_lesions` licence must be verified before deployment**, directly in the README of the version being installed.

**Kidney.** The KiTS23 winner (NVIDIA, an ensemble of 15 networks from MONAI Auto3DSeg, Dice 0.835) would be the most MONAI-native choice, but **no downloadable checkpoint exists** ([arXiv 2310.04110](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.04110); [GitHub neheller/kits23](https://github.com/neheller/kits23)). For kidney tumors, ULS+ and partly the TotalSegmentator `kidney_cysts` task remain.

**Blank spots.** For **bone metastases** no mature detector with open weights was found for CT, MRI or scintigraphy. Published models for the spine (Dice 0.70–0.82) and scintigraphy (AUC about 0.94–0.96) did not release weights ([PMC11399709](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11399709); [PMC7428190](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7428190/)). For **lymph nodes**, the LNQ2023 challenge produced a best median Dice of 71.2 % (DKFZ), but with no link to code or weights, and TotalSegmentator has no lymph node class ([arXiv 2408.10069](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10069)). For **head and neck on PET/CT**, only the old HECKTOR 2020 winner's weights are verified, and they cover only GTVp ([arXiv 2102.10446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.10446)). In all three cases it is worth combining TotalSegmentator bone masks with LesionTracer (PET/CT) or with ULS+/LesionLocator (CT). The LNQ winner's recipe (nnU-Net ResEnc trained on TCIA CT Lymph Nodes and weak LNQ labels) can be reproduced on public data only, without local patients.

## Treatment response follow-up can be assembled from open parts

No open, ready-made "RECIST workflow" Slicer extension was found. The building blocks exist, though. ULS23 and ULS+ are also evaluated on the long and short axis, so their masks directly yield RECIST-style diameters ([ULS23 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05231v2)). LesionLocator_track autoregressively propagates masks from the baseline study to follow-ups and provides volumetric follow-up ([arXiv 2502.20985](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20985)). Public data exist for validation: a CT dataset with RECIST measurements and tumor and lymph node masks, where lesion IDs stay the same across studies ([Nature Sci Data 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-06597-6)), and a longitudinal whole-body CT dataset with lesion-tracking metadata ([PMC13443671](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13443671/)).

A natural workflow in MOONAI would look like this. A click in OHIF runs ULS+ or LesionLocator and produces a mask. The long and short axis and the volume are computed from the mask. The result is stored as DICOM SEG/SR in Orthanc. On the follow-up study, LesionLocator_track runs. The end result is a per-lesion change table with RECIST 1.1 thresholds (+20 % / −30 %) and the volume change. Because of the prompt-model instability mentioned above, the rollout should include a reproducibility test: the same lesion, different clicks, variance of diameter and volume. The commercial reference for this workflow is **mint Lesion** (Mint Medical), which supports RECIST and other criteria and is used in clinical reading and pharmaceutical trials ([GUDID](https://accessgudid.nlm.nih.gov/devices/04260495880372)).

## TotalSegmentator v2 should replace the old anatomy bundles

In CT and MR anatomy, the biggest and least risky improvement is promoting TotalSegmentator from reference to production segmenter. Version 2.12+ has **117 CT classes** in the `total` task, **50 MR classes** in `total_mr` and about 45 subtasks. Of these, **31 are Apache-2.0**, including `liver_segments(_mr)`, `lung_vessels`, `vertebrae_mr`, `head_glands_cavities`, `headneck_muscles` and `pleural_pericard_effusion` ([GitHub wasserth/TotalSegmentator](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator)). Useful tools were added too, e.g. `totalseg_get_phase` to determine the contrast phase, `totalseg_get_body_stats`, JSON output via `--report` and an MCP server. The MR version reached Dice 0.839 on 80 structures in Radiology 2025 ([RSNA Radiology](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.250843)). Benchmarks between groups contradict each other, however. The authors of **MRSegmentator v2** (Charité, 40 structures plus 10 body-composition classes, code Apache-2.0, weight licence unstated) report comparable performance and better results on NAKO and AMOS ([MRSegmentator arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06463)). It is therefore sensible to use MRSegmentator as a second opinion on MR.

In practice this means **wholeBody_ct_segmentation** (the old TotalSegmentator v1 label set) and **swin_unetr_btcv** (13 organs) become redundant. pancreas_ct_dints is worth keeping only if it shows better results on local data. MR body anatomy, which MOONAI lacks today (it has only prostate and brain), is added through `total_mr`, `vertebrae_mr` and `liver_segments_mr`. For radiotherapy, the open TotalSegmentator subtasks cover many organs at risk (OAR) of the head and neck, muscles, bones and vessels. They are not validated against RT protocols, though, so dosimetric acceptability must be verified locally.

**NV-Segment-CTMR** (successor of VISTA3D, October 2025) brings more than 345 classes on CT and MRI, including 133 brain substructures, in MONAI bundle format. It could therefore replace wholeBrainSeg_UNEST. Sources contradict each other on the weight licence, however: the repository README speaks of a commercially friendly "NVIDIA open models license", while the comparison table links the NVIDIA One-Way Noncommercial License ([GitHub Project-MONAI/VISTA](https://github.com/Project-MONAI/VISTA/tree/main/vista3d)). The licence file of the specific checkpoint must be checked. Foundation models such as CT-FM, nnFoundation or STU-Net only make sense once MOONAI starts fine-tuning. Text-prompted models such as SAT need 24–34 GB VRAM and lag behind specialized models, e.g. Dice 38.45 versus 54.39 for nnU-Net on MSD Colon ([arXiv 2312.17183](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.17183)).

## Mammography, X-ray and VLMs: useful additions, not the main priority

**Mammography.** Two open models are usable today, both MIT-licensed. **Mirai** predicts breast cancer risk at 1 to 5 years from 4 views. In a multi-institutional validation at 7 sites in 5 countries it reached a C-index of 0.75–0.84 ([PubMed 34767469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34767469/)) and ships as the Docker service `mitjclinic/mirai` ([GitHub Mirai](https://github.com/reginabarzilaygroup/Mirai)). It was, however, trained exclusively on Hologic machines, and on images from another vendor (IMS) the C-index dropped to **0.55** ([Insights into Imaging 2024](https://doaj.org/article/adc2bcf5d9274e95ba75cef9e8a7a830)). The second is the **RSNA Kaggle 2023 winning ensemble**, which outputs a cancer probability without localization ([GitHub dangnh0611](https://github.com/dangnh0611/kaggle_rsna_breast_cancer)). NYU's GMIC provides saliency maps for localization but is AGPL-3.0. The newer foundation models Mammo-CLIP and Mammo-FM have non-commercial licences, and Mammo-FM explicitly forbids clinical deployment as well as **hosting via API**. That could also affect serving it from the MONAI Label server ([HF batmanLab/Mammo-FM](https://huggingface.co/batmanLab/Mammo-FM)). Certified products are ahead on evidence: Transpara reached AUC 0.960 on DM ([J Breast Imaging 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jbi/wbag042)) and has the MASAI RCT. No open-versus-commercial comparison on the same data exists yet.

**Chest and X-ray.** The quickest win is **TorchXRayVision**: 18 pathologies including the Nodule, Mass and Lung Lesion classes, segmentation of 14 anatomical structures, pip install and runs on CPU too ([GitHub mlmed/torchxrayvision](https://github.com/mlmed/torchxrayvision)). These are classifier probabilities, though, not localized detections, and the licence was not verified in this research. No open chest X-ray nodule detector with a clear licence was found. Open fracture models are trained mostly on pediatric wrists (GRAZPEDWRI-DX) and have weak external validation ([JMAI](https://jmai.amegroups.org/article/view/11415/html)). They are marginal for oncology.

**VLMs for reports.** For 3D CT the strongest on paper is **NV-Reason-CT** (NVIDIA, 4.7B, native 3D ViT, NIfTI input in HU). On CT-RATE it reached Macro-F1 0.614 versus 0.303 for MedGemma 1.5. These are NVIDIA's own numbers, though; the model is at version 0.1, English only, and research only (OpenMDW-1.1) ([HF nvidia/NV-Reason-CT](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NV-Reason-CT)). The safest in licence terms is **Merlin** (Stanford, Nature 2026, MIT) for abdominal CT ([HF stanfordmimi/Merlin](https://huggingface.co/stanfordmimi/Merlin)). For chest X-ray, Lingshu (MIT) and MAIRA-2 (MSRLA, research only) are candidates. The key finding supports MOONAI's current architecture. In a blinded evaluation of real emergency-department chest X-rays, open VLMs had clinically significant errors in **16.8–43 %** of reports and hallucinated in **5.4–17.4 %**, whereas radiologists hallucinated in 0.1 % ([arXiv 2512.00271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00271)). A design where code computes the measurements and the LLM only verbalizes them is therefore safer. BTReport takes a similar approach, explicitly forbidding the LLM from stating findings that cannot be derived from the computed features ([arXiv 2602.16006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16006)). VLM output belongs in a separate, clearly labeled "AI suggestion, unverified" section. No published evaluation of a medical LLM writing radiology reports in Slovak or Czech was found. Slovak text should therefore be generated from structured findings by a strong multilingual LLM (e.g. from the Qwen3 family), with a fixed template and glossary.

## Commercial products are certified but do not replace a research platform

Mature CE-marked products exist for every area. In mammography these are Transpara and Lunit; for chest X-ray Annalise (124 findings, class IIb), Qure.ai, Lunit and the Czech Carebot; for fractures Gleamer BoneView; for CT triage Aidoc; and for oncology response follow-up mint Lesion ([ScreenPoint](https://screenpoint-medical.com/insights/transpara-regulatory-clearances); [SoftwareOne Annalise](https://platform.softwareone.com/product/annalise-enterprise-cxr/PCP-6441-4029)). In Slovakia, Penta Hospitals has used Gleamer and Rayscape for X-ray in all 19 facilities since autumn 2025 ([SITA](https://sita.sk/vzdravotnictve/umela-inteligencia-podporuje-rtg-diagnostiku-vo-vsetkych-zariadeniach-siete-penta-hospitals-slovensko/)). No deployment of commercial radiology AI at Slovak oncology institutes was found. Of the platforms, the most relevant is Germany's **deepcOS**, whose owner deepc also owns Osimis, the company behind Orthanc's commercial line. It offers more than 50 apps from over 30 vendors, but inference runs in the cloud on pseudonymized data ([deepc.ai](https://deepc.ai/deploy)). The only public price is £5,000 to £150,000 per year ([UK Digital Marketplace](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-14/services/839252441331899)). Bayer's shutdown of the Calantic and Blackford platforms in 2025 shows that platform lock-in risk is real ([AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/imaging-informatics/artificial-intelligence/article/15754790/bayer-ag-bayer-pivots-away-from-ai-platform-business)).

Architecturally, a commercial gateway could act as another DICOM node next to Orthanc and return results as SR/SEG for OHIF to display. This is an inference, not a documented integration. Regulatorily it matters: if the hospital uses an open model for a clinical decision, it becomes a manufacturer under the MDR. The in-house exemption under MDR Art. 5(5) requires justification that no equivalent CE device exists, which is hard to defend for mammography or X-ray. The notes do not cite this rule from a primary source, so treat it as an orientation. The AI Act classifies medical AI as high-risk. According to reports on the Digital Omnibus, obligations for medical devices are postponed to 2 August 2028 at the latest, but the final text in the Official Journal was not verified ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/12/eu-digital-omnibus-implications-for-medtech-companies); [MedDeviceGuide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-amendment-2026-medical-device-impact-guide)). A sensible split is therefore: buy certified modules for clinical use, and keep MOONAI for research, validation and areas where no certified product exists, such as lesion volumetry and longitudinal follow-up.

## Prioritized recommendation: what to add to MOONAI first

The ordering considers oncology benefit, weight maturity, ease of integration into MONAI Label/OHIF and licence risk. Most new models are built on nnU-Net v2, so the integration pattern is the same: a custom MONAI Label InferTask calling `nnUNetPredictor`, or a separate Docker service writing DICOM SEG into Orthanc.

| Priority | What to add | Why | Licence (non-commercial research in the EU) | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **TotalSegmentator v2 as production segmenter** (CT + MR; retire wholeBody_ct and swin_unetr_btcv) | Cheapest and broadest improvement; MR anatomy, Couinaud segments, `lung_nodules` | Apache-2.0 for 31 tasks; licensed tasks free for academia | **Verify the `liver_lesions` licence** (notes disagree) |
| 2 | **ULS+** as "click and measure" (RECIST) in OHIF | Best open universal lesion segmentation model (Dice 0.78), Apache code | Code Apache-2.0; **weight licence unverified** | Not a detector; sensitive to click position |
| 3 | **nnInteractive** as an interactive service next to MONAI Label | Ready client for OHIF and Slicer; any tumor or OAR; CT/MR/PET | Weights CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | About 10 GB VRAM; unusable if commercialized |
| 4 | **LesionLocator_track** for follow-up | The only open longitudinal lesion-tracking model with weights | Code GPL-3.0, weights CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | 14 GB checkpoint, high RAM |
| 5 | **LesionTracer** for whole-body PET/CT | autoPET III winner; automatic FDG/PSMA lesion detection | **CC BY 4.0** | Weaker for PSMA than FDG |
| 6 | **BrainLesion `brats`** instead of the old brats bundle | Metastases and meningiomas, not just gliomas | Code Apache-2.0, containers unstated | Docker; all 4 sequences required |
| 7 | **PI-CAI** (prostate) and **Sybil** (LDCT risk) | Strongest multi-center second-reader evidence | PI-CAI weights unstated; Sybil MIT | PI-CAI Siemens/Philips only; Sybil LDCT only |
| 8 | **MAMA-MIA**, **PanDx**, **Mirai** depending on research projects | Breast MRI, PDAC, mammography risk | CC BY-NC 4.0 / unverified / MIT | US data; Hologic |

The first four items form one coherent oncology package: automatic anatomy, click-to-measure lesions, interactive correction and follow-up. Together they cover the full RECIST cycle without any training on local patients. Before use in a research study each model should run in **silent mode on 50–200 retrospective local cases**. The output should be sensitivity and false positives per study, because validation on Central European data is missing for all of these models. On licences: **CC BY 4.0 and MIT/Apache** (ULS23, LesionTracer, Sybil, Mirai, the open TotalSegmentator tasks) have no practical restrictions beyond attribution. **CC BY-NC(-SA)** (LesionLocator, nnInteractive, MAMA-MIA) is fine for non-commercial research, but fine-tuned derivatives inherit share-alike and must not become part of a commercial product or spin-off. For weights without a stated licence (ULS+, PI-CAI, the `brats` containers, PanDx), find the licence or ask the authors, and until then assume "all rights reserved". **None of the open models is CE-certified under the MDR.** TotalSegmentator explicitly describes itself as "not a medical device", and the VISTA3D model card says "for research purposes and not for clinical usage" ([TotalSegmentator](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator); [NVIDIA VISTA-3D](https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/vista-3d/modelcard)). All outputs in OHIF must be visibly labeled as research and must not be used for clinical decisions.

## Conclusion

The quality of individual oncology models has improved over the past two years. Even more important is the shift in how they are used: from automatic organ segmentation to interactive tools, where the radiologist points at a lesion and the model delivers a precise 3D mask and its evolution over time. MOONAI is well positioned for this shift, because OHIF and 3D Slicer are already in the system and the new models share the common nnU-Net foundation. The platform's value thus moves from the number of bundles to the quality of the response-measurement workflow and to local validation, which no one else will do for the institute.

The biggest risk today is not technical but licensing and regulatory. The best interactive models (nnInteractive, LesionLocator, MedSAM2) are non-commercial. For several key models the weight licence is not stated at all, and one TotalSegmentator licence changed between checks. If the institute ever considers clinical deployment or a spin-off, it pays to keep a model register now, with version, source and weight licence. Where possible, prefer models under CC BY 4.0 and Apache-2.0 (TotalSegmentator, ULS23, LesionTracer). Bone metastases, lymph nodes and head and neck remain areas where the institute can contribute its own research: training on public data following the challenge winners' recipes, without using local patients.
